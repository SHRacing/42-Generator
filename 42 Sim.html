How the simulation works
  • Deal. All 28 dominoes are shuffled and each player draws 7. The shaker rotates every hand.
  • Bidding. One round, starting left of the shaker. Each player sees only their own hand. For every contract the rules allow, they deal the other 21 dominoes at random many times and play each layout out to measure their odds. They bid the highest level they make at least as often as the threshold, choose the contract with the best expected marks, and only overbid their partner with a clearly better contract.
  • Play. Every player follows suit, tracks what has been played and who is out of a suit, pulls trump, cashes boss dominoes, smears count onto a partner's sure trick, and fights for every trick in contracts that need all seven. Nello players duck under the bidder. Nobody sees anyone else's hand.
  • Spot. Every player must play the domino whose pips are closest to the spot number. The closest domino wins the trick and ties go to whoever played first. The bidder leads, and the bidding team must take every trick.
  • Scoring. A made point bid scores 1 mark; a made mark bid scores its marks. A set gives those marks to the opponents.
  • Limits. The play is strong rule-based play, not a perfect solver, and every odds estimate comes from a finite number of look-ahead deals. The Bid accuracy chart shows how well those estimates hold up.
